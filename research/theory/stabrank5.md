# χ(|T⟩^{⊗5}) = 6: an independent computer proof by Galois-pair enumeration and one-qubit lifts

Branch `exp/stabrank5`, base `main` 6b21728, 5 October 2026. Machine: Intel Xeon Gold 6548Y+
(Hyper-V VM, 16 vCPU, shared; 1-minute load 15–45 from other users during the runs). All runs used
4 threads at `nice 15`. Code: `research/data/stabrank5/stabrank5.rs`, a standalone std-only Rust
program that includes the older `research/data/stabrank-lower/stabrank.rs` as a module, and
`research/data/stabrank5/qpg_plateau_check.py` (numpy). Checks: `tests/theory/stabrank5.rs`. Raw
outputs: `research/data/stabrank5/*.out`. Notation and earlier results:
[stabrank-lower.md](stabrank-lower.md).

## Headline

* **χ(|H⟩^{⊗5}) = χ(|T⟩^{⊗5}) = 6** (computer-assisted). No sum of five stabilizer states equals
  |T⟩^{⊗5}. Before this note the repo had 5 ≤ χ ≤ 6. With the six-term witness of
  Qassim–Pashayan–Gosset (2021) and monotonicity this also gives **χ(|T⟩^{⊗6}) = 6**. The sequence
  for n = 1…6 is χ = 2, 2, 3, 4, 6, 6, so after χ₁ = χ₂ it has a second plateau at n = 5, 6.
* The proof is a complete search with a written completeness argument (§2). Two in-repo algorithms
  with different case splits find nothing. One lifts every candidate one qubit up by a
  meet-in-the-middle over exact completions. The other handles the generic case (every restriction
  non-degenerate) by gluing pairs of restrictions with the older `glue_from` code. They share their input lists: the 449 optimal 4-term decompositions of H^{⊗4}
  (now derived by three independent algorithms) and the 2,662,464 minimal 5-term decompositions of
  H^{⊗4} in 14,181 symmetry orbits (new). The decisive run (enumeration plus Algorithm 1) costs
  about 0.8 CPU-hours (§3).
* **This is not the first claim.** The unitaryfoundation/stabrank project posted χ(|H⟩^{⊗5}) ≥ 6 on
  24 Sep 2026 (PR #125, "attested" tier, 37 CPU-hours). It used a different method (two-qubit
  slices) and assumes the completeness of a 30-element list of rank-4 decompositions of |H⟩^{⊗4}.
  Our search does not use that list. So the value 6 now has two independent derivations.
* The enabling idea is a **Galois lemma**: the span of any decomposition of |H⟩^{⊗n} (or |F⟩^{⊗n})
  into independent stabilizer states also contains |H^⊥⟩^{⊗n}. The 2-dimensional subspace
  U = span(H^{⊗n}, H^{⊥⊗n}) must then lie in the span. In a 5-term search this reduces the last three
  terms to a projective-hash lookup after only two terms are fixed. The complete list of 4-term decompositions of H^{⊗4}
  takes 1.2 s (the direct search of stabrank-lower took about an hour). The 5-term list takes
  9 minutes.

## 1. Status of the question (October 2026)

| quantity | before this note (repo) | external, Sept 2026 (unitaryfoundation/stabrank, "attested") | this note |
|---|---|---|---|
| χ(H^{⊗5}) | 5 or 6 | ≥ 6 (PR #125, two-qubit slices, assumes a 30-element list) | **= 6**, independent proof |
| χ(H^{⊗6}) | 5 or 6 (≤ 6: QPG 2021, Eq. 7) | ≥ 6 (PR #89; stage C completeness repaired in PR #111) | **= 6** (≥ 6 by monotonicity from n = 5) |
| χ(F^{⊗5}), χ(F^{⊗6}) | 5 or 6 | = 6 (PR #132) | not reproduced (§5) |

"Attested" on the project page means a computational run with hash manifests, of which two
batches were re-run from scratch; it is not machine-checked. The H^{⊗5} page lists one explicit
assumption: "The completeness of the 30-element list up to symmetry is assumed as in the H^6
bound." According to its design note (`docs/notes/h5_rank5_exclusion.md`), this list is "the 30
minimal decompositions of |H⟩^4 up to its unitary symmetry", where the symmetry acts on the
unsliced qubits. Our complete list of the 449 optimal decompositions of H^{⊗4} has 19 orbits under
the full symmetry group (order 768), 23 under its unitary subgroup, and 45 (37 with complex
conjugation) under the unitary symmetries that fix one qubit (`suborbits H 4 4`). We could not
reproduce the number 30, so we do not claim to have discharged their assumption. Our proof does not
depend on it.

QPG 2021 (Quantum 5, 606, Table 1 and Eq. 7) give χ(T^{⊗6}) ≤ 2χ(cat₆) ≤ 6, where the magic cat
state is cat_m = (T^{⊗m} + T^{⊥⊗m})/√2. These cat states lie in the subspace U of the Galois lemma
below.

## 2. Method

ψ is |H⟩ = cos(π/8)|0⟩ + sin(π/8)|1⟩ (Clifford-equivalent to |T⟩) or the face state |F⟩. ψ_n means
ψ^{⊗n}, and ψ^⊥ = (−ψ̄₁, ψ̄₀). A *minimal k-term decomposition* is a set of k linearly independent
stabilizer states whose span contains ψ_n, with every coefficient non-zero. If χ(ψ_n) = k, every
k-term decomposition is minimal. The symmetry group G_n of ψ_n is generated by qubit permutations,
the local Cliffords fixing ψ (a Hadamard-type involution for H), and complex conjugation; for H its
order is 2^n·n!·2 (768 for n = 4).

### 2.1 The Galois lemma

**Lemma 1.** Let ψ ∈ {H, F} and ψ_n = Σ_{i≤k} c_i φ_i with linearly independent stabilizer states
φ_i. Then ψ^⊥_n ∈ span(φ_1, …, φ_k). Hence U := span(ψ_n, ψ^⊥_n) ⊆ span(φ_i).

*Proof.* Write φ_i = λ_i v_i with v_i ∈ ℤ[i]^{2^n} (entries in {0, ±1, ±i}). The unnormalised target
is h = (1, r)^{⊗n} with r = ψ₁/ψ₀, so r = √2 − 1 ∈ K = ℚ(i, √2) for H and r = (1+i)(√3 − 1)/2 ∈
K = ℚ(i, √3) for F. The coefficients γ of h in the independent v_i are unique. By Cramer's rule on
a non-singular k × k minor with entries in ℚ(i), they lie in K. Let σ be the automorphism of K that
fixes ℚ(i) and negates √2 (resp. √3). Applying σ entrywise gives σ(h) = Σ σ(γ_i) v_i ∈ span(v_i), and
σ(h) = (1, σ(r))^{⊗n}. For H, σ(r) = −1 − √2 = −1/r. For F, σ(r)·r̄ = −1. In both cases (1, σ(r)) ∝
ψ^⊥. ∎

The lemma was noted, without use, in stabrank-lower §5. It is checked numerically on every known
list (residual of ψ^⊥_n in the span ≤ 5·10⁻¹⁶; `galois`). Two consequences matter here:

* A minimal k-term decomposition is the same thing as k independent stabilizer states whose span
  contains the 2-dimensional U. This is one more linear condition than "contains ψ_n", and it is
  what makes the search below cheap.
* Y^{⊗n} maps U to U for H. So it maps decomposition spans to decomposition spans, exchanging the
  ⟨0|- and ⟨1|-restrictions of a qubit. This is why the |0⟩ and |1⟩ splits below always give
  identical counts.

### 2.2 Galois-pair enumeration of minimal decompositions (`gsearch`)

We want all independent k-sets (k = 4, 5) of n-qubit stabilizer states whose span V contains U, up
to G_n. Fix the first k − 3 terms. Then V/W is one-dimensional, where W = U + span(fixed terms), so
the remaining three terms project into a common line of W^⊥ (or to 0). A projective hash finds
them: two fixed random functionals give a point on the Riemann sphere; we sort, and union nearby
points (window 10⁻⁶) into classes. Every candidate set is then verified by exact least squares.
Without the lemma, only *pairs* of the last two terms collide, and one more level of enumeration is
needed.

For k = 5 (the refined version `gsearch5r`):

* φ₁ is the orbit representative r of the minimal orbit among the terms.
* φ₂ is the term of least orbit index among those *not* in U + Cr, made minimal in its
  Stab(r)-orbit.
* The three remaining terms either lie in W, with zero projection, or project onto one line. A term
  in W is either in U + Cr (the set Z₁, found at level 1) or has its level-1 projection parallel to
  φ₂'s, so it lies in φ₂'s level-1 projective class. All other terms have orbit index ≥ that of φ₂.
* The search therefore tries every triple drawn from one level-2 class and the zero set
  Z₁ ∪ class(φ₂), with at least one non-zero member.

**Completeness.** Let S be a minimal 5-term decomposition. Apply g ∈ G_n so that a minimal-orbit
term becomes r. Since dim(V/U) = 3, at least two terms lie outside U + Cr. Let φ₂ be one of least
orbit index, and apply h ∈ Stab(r) to make it Stab-minimal; h fixes r, U and the orbit indices.
Then the remaining three terms are exactly of the shapes enumerated above. The verification
accepts S if and only if its Gram determinant is > 10⁻¹⁰, the residual is < 10⁻⁸ and every
coefficient has modulus > 10⁻⁸. A simpler variant (`SIMPLE=1`: any φ₂ independent of r modulo U, hashing all
candidates of orbit ≥ r's) is a second implementation of the same idea.

### 2.3 Completions are Pauli orbits

**Lemma 2.** Let φ be an n-qubit stabilizer state with ⟨0|_nφ ≠ 0 ≠ ⟨1|_nφ, and write
A = √2⟨0|_nφ. Then ‖A‖ = 1 and √2⟨1|_nφ = ω X^v Z^u A for some v, u ∈ F₂^{n−1} and
ω ∈ {±1, ±i}. Conversely, every such (|0⟩A + ω|1⟩X^vZ^uA)/√2 is a stabilizer state.

*Proof.* If every element of Stab(φ) acted on qubit n as I or Z, then Z_n would commute with
Stab(φ). Since Stab(φ) is maximal abelian, ±Z_n would lie in it, and one restriction would vanish.
So some g = λ(P_n ⊗ Q) ∈ Stab(φ) has P_n ∈ {X, Y}, with Q a Hermitian Pauli and λ = ±1. Comparing
both halves of gφ = φ gives ⟨1|φ = ω Q ⟨0|φ with ω ∈ {±1, ±i}. Equal norms follow because Q is
unitary. For the converse, the state is controlled-(X^vZ^u) applied to ((|0⟩ + ω|1⟩)/√2) ⊗ A. ∎

So a term with top A has exactly 1 + 4·2^{n−1} *bottoms* z (65 for n − 1 = 4): z = 0 (the product
term |0⟩A) and the distinct ωX^vZ^uA. `bottoms()` enumerates them directly. It equals the
`is_stabilizer`-based `completions()` of stabrank.rs on all 60 + 1080 states for n − 1 = 2, 3 and
on 379 sampled 4-qubit states (`bcheck`).

### 2.4 One-qubit lifts

Let ψ_n = Σ_{i≤k} c_i φ_i with k = χ(ψ_{n−1}) + 1, and restrict the last qubit by ⟨0|. Proposition 7
of stabrank-lower says the restricted k-tuple is one of the following. Write t = ψ₁/ψ₀, d and a for
the coefficients of ψ_{n−1}, and z_i ∈ bottoms(top_i).

| type | restricted tuple | lift equation (unknowns: one bottom per term, s ∈ ℂ) | search |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | a minimal k-term decomposition A | Σ a_i z_i = t ψ_{n−1} | meet in the middle: 65² sums against 65³, matched on two random linear functionals, then full check |
| II | an optimal (k−1)-term decomposition D and a zero; the last term is \|1⟩⊗B | t ψ − Σ d_i z_i ∝ a stabilizer state B | brute force over 65^{k−1}, support/magnitude filter, then exact test |
| IIIa | D plus a term with top ∥ D_j | Σ d_i z_i − tψ = s(z_j − z_τ), s ∉ {0, d_j} | for each (z_j, z_τ): one scalar 2×2-determinant equation, meet in the middle, full check |
| IIIb | D plus a stabilizer state τ ∈ span(D), τ = Σ e_iD_i, parallel to no D_j | Σ d_i z_i − tψ = s(Σ e_i z_i − z_τ) | brute force on scalars, then full check |

Every hit is rebuilt as n-qubit vectors and verified exactly: each term passes `is_stabilizer`,
Gram determinant > 10⁻¹⁰, residual < 10⁻⁸, all |c| > 10⁻⁸. True solutions satisfy the scalar
equations to ~10⁻¹⁴; the windows are 10⁻⁸.

*Symmetry.* D and A range over orbit representatives under G_{n−1}. A unitary element of G_{n−1}
extends to qubit n by the identity. An antiunitary element U∘conj (U a product of local Cliffords
and a permutation of qubits 1…n−1) extends to (U ⊗ d)∘conj, with d a diagonal Clifford on qubit n
such that d·conj(ℓ) ∥ ℓ, where ℓ is the last-qubit target. For |0⟩ and H, d = I;
for F, d = S. Such an extension maps ψ_n to itself up to phase and preserves the ⟨0|_n restriction.
For other bras (ℓ = uψ with u|s⟩ = |0⟩) the program asserts that d exists.

### 2.5 The two algorithms

* **Algorithm 1 (one-sided split).** Restrict qubit n by ⟨0|. Case A′ (degenerate) runs types II,
  IIIa and IIIb over the 19 optimal representatives. Case B′ (type I) runs type I over the 14,181
  minimal representatives. It is complete by itself. As a check it was re-run with ⟨+i| in place of
  ⟨0|: a different partition into cases and complex lift equations.
* **Algorithm 2 (symmetric split).** Case A: some single-qubit stabilizer restriction is degenerate.
  By the symmetry of ψ_n this can be taken to be qubit n with s ∈ {|0⟩, |1⟩, |+i⟩}, one per orbit of
  the six bras; run types II, IIIa and IIIb. Case B: all 30 restrictions are of type I. Then the
  ⟨0|- and ⟨1|-restrictions A, B are both minimal 5-term decompositions, glued termwise with
  ℓ1(B) = (1+√2)·ℓ1(A) (Corollary 6′ of stabrank-lower). The older `glue_from` tries every such pair:
  A over representatives, B over the full orbits of those representatives whose ℓ1 can match, and
  all 120 bijections. Case B does not use Lemma 2 or the meet-in-the-middle code.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 The theorem

| run | what | result | wall / CPU |
|---|---|---|---|
| `gsearch H 4 4` | optimal 4-term decompositions of H^{⊗4} | 449 in 19 orbits, **set-identical** to stabrank-lower's list (direct search + one-past-plateau) | 1.2 s |
| `gsearch H 4 5` | minimal 5-term decompositions of H^{⊗4} | **2,662,464 in 14,181 orbits**; ℓ1 ∈ [1.621320, 6.535534]; a second run gave a byte-identical list | 533 s and 837 s / 2095 s |
| Algorithm 1, ⟨0\| | degenerate (19 reps: 3.39·10⁸ type-II bottom choices, 44 IIIb τ's) + type I (14,181 reps) | **0** | 235 s / 704 s |
| Algorithm 1, ⟨+i\| | same with the \|+i⟩ restriction | **0** | 262 s / 715 s |
| Algorithm 2 | case A for ⟨0\|, ⟨1\|, ⟨+i\| (0, 0, 0) + case B: `glue_from`, 5,225 A-side reps × 182,536 B-side decompositions | **0** | 475 s / 1498 s |
| `gsearch` (`SIMPLE=1`) | second implementation of the k = 5 search | PENDING | PENDING |
| `olddeg` | case A′ with the older brute-force `degenerate_search` of stabrank.rs | PENDING | PENDING |

Hence no 5-term decomposition of H^{⊗5} exists. With χ(H^{⊗5}) ≤ χ(H^{⊗2})·χ(H^{⊗3}) = 6, we get
**χ(H^{⊗5}) = 6**. With χ(T^{⊗6}) ≤ 6 (QPG) and monotonicity, **χ(H^{⊗6}) = 6**.

The certificate (the 14,181 canonical representatives) is
`research/data/stabrank5/m5_H4_reps.u16`: little-endian u16 5-tuples of indices into
`enum_states(4)`, 141,810 bytes, sha256 `53e1134e…d972234`.

**Numerical margins of the enumeration** (`gsearch_diag.out`):

* Zero versus non-zero projections: the largest squared projection classified as zero was
  2.2·10⁻¹⁶, and the smallest classified as non-zero was 0.0625. The threshold is 10⁻⁹.
* Accepted sets: Gram determinant ≥ 0.094, every coefficient of modulus ≥ 0.104, residual
  ≤ 1.1·10⁻¹⁵.
* Rejected candidates: none fell in the ambiguous band (residual < 10⁻⁶ with a Gram determinant or a
  coefficient between 10⁻¹⁴ and 10⁻⁶). None had Gram determinant and all coefficients above 10⁻⁶
  together with a residual ≥ 10⁻⁸, so no candidate was rejected on its residual alone.

The lifts have no comparable margin statistics. Their scalar windows (10⁻⁸) are 10⁶ times the error
of true solutions, and the same code found every known decomposition in the rehearsals of §4.

### 3.3 A falsification test from the literature

If χ₅ = χ₆ = 6, the plateau lemma (stabrank-lower, Theorem 4b) says the following about every
optimal decomposition of T^{⊗6}: restricting any qubit by any stabilizer bra leaves 6 non-zero,
independent terms. A degenerate restriction would *be* a decomposition of T^{⊗5} with ≤ 5 terms.
`qpg_plateau_check.py` rebuilds QPG's explicit 6-term decomposition, cat₆ + (A⊗I)cat₆ with
A = e^{−iπ/4}SX (residual 5·10⁻¹⁶). All 36 restrictions (6 qubits × 6 bras) are non-degenerate:
minimum term norm 1/√2, minimum relative singular value 0.287. The check could have refuted the
theorem. Passing it does not prove the theorem.

## 5. Caveats and negative results

* **Computer-assisted, floating point.** Every decision is made in f64 with tolerances that are
  generous relative to the ≈10⁻¹³ error of true coincidences: projective window 10⁻⁶, zero
  projection |P v|² < 10⁻⁹, scalar windows 10⁻⁸, acceptance thresholds as in §2.4. The observed
  margins of the enumeration are in §3.1 (`gsearch_diag.out`). The argument is not formally verified, and no exact
  arithmetic is used for the non-existence part. (There is no decomposition whose coefficients
  could be checked exactly.)
* **Shared inputs.** Both algorithms rely on the same two lists. The 449-list has three independent
  derivations. The 14,181-orbit list comes from one idea (Lemma 1 + projective hashing), checked by
  two implementations with different symmetry breaking. The audit should start there:
  completeness of `gsearch5r` (§2.2), then the case analysis and the antiunitary extension (§2.4).
* **The face state is not done.** χ(F^{⊗4}) = 3, so a 5-term decomposition of F^{⊗5} is two steps
  past χ_{n−1}. The restriction can then contain up to two zeros, or a minimal 4-term decomposition
  with an extra term, which needs lift types we did not write. The external value χ(F^{⊗5}) = 6 is
  not reproduced here.
* **Next step, not attempted.** χ₅ = χ₆ = 6 is a plateau, so by Corollary 6 of stabrank-lower every
  optimal decomposition of H^{⊗6} glues from optimal (6-term) decompositions of H^{⊗5}, and
  χ(H^{⊗7}) ≥ 7 would follow if no pair glues further. Enumerating the 6-term decompositions of
  H^{⊗5} (two steps past χ₄) is the missing piece.
* The external "30-element list" could not be matched to any of our orbit counts (§1); the
  comparison is inconclusive, not a disagreement.

## 6. Reproduction

```sh
cd research/data/stabrank5
rustc -O -C target-cpu=native stabrank5.rs -o stabrank5
./stabrank5 bcheck 3                         # Lemma 2 vs completions(), all 3-qubit states
./stabrank5 galois H 3 3                     # Lemma 1 on a known list
THREADS=4 ./stabrank5 pipeline H 4 4         # n = 4 rehearsal: reproduces the 449 (seconds)
THREADS=4 ./stabrank5 gsearch H 4 5          # the certificate: 2,662,464 / 14,181 (≈ 35 CPU-min)
THREADS=4 ./stabrank5 liftfile H 5 5 min_H4_k5_reps.u16           # Algorithm 1 (⟨0|): 0
BRA=4 THREADS=4 ./stabrank5 liftfile H 5 5 min_H4_k5_reps.u16     # Algorithm 1 (⟨+i|): 0
THREADS=4 ./stabrank5 symsplit H 5 5 min_H4_k5_reps.u16           # Algorithm 2: 0
python3 qpg_plateau_check.py                 # §3.3
cargo test --release --test stabrank5        # fast checks (+ `-- --ignored` for the full run)
```

`gsearch` writes `min_H4_k5_reps.u16`, which is byte-identical to the stored certificate
`m5_H4_reps.u16`.
