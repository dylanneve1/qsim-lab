# The free-fermion (Gaussian) engine

`qsim_lab::engines::gaussian` finds free-fermion structure in an arbitrary
gate list and simulates circuits that have it in polynomial time. The planner
calls it `Engine::Gaussian` (`"gauss"`); in Python it is
`engine="gaussian"`, and `qsimlab.analysis.gaussian()` returns the detector's
report.

It was written after the SU(2) hadron-dynamics tracker circuits (120 qubits,
[research/data/su2-hadron](../research/data/su2-hadron/README.md)) turned
out to be nearly free fermions. Once the SWAP network is undone, every
two-qubit block is either an exact number-conserving Gaussian gate or a
diagonal gate with a small interaction phase (|g| ≤ 0.01). The detector
finds this without being told about chains, sites or angles.

## 1. The math

**Jordan–Wigner.** Fix an order of the wires, the *modes* `k = 0..n-1`.
The Majorana operators are

    γ_{2k}   = Z_0 … Z_{k-1} X_k
    γ_{2k+1} = Z_0 … Z_{k-1} Y_k

They are Hermitian and satisfy `{γ_a, γ_b} = 2δ_ab`. `Z_k = -i γ_{2k} γ_{2k+1}`
is local in every order. `X_k X_{k+1}`, `X_k Y_{k+1}`, `Y_k X_{k+1}` and
`Y_k Y_{k+1}` are quadratic in the γ only when `k` and `k+1` are adjacent in
the order.

**Gaussian unitaries.** `U` is Gaussian (a free-fermion gate) if it maps the
span of the Majorana operators to itself: `U† γ_a U = Σ_b Q_ab γ_b` with `Q`
in `O(2n)`. Generated by quadratic Hamiltonians, these are the `SO(2n)` part.
Parity-flipping maps such as `X_k` (a diagonal `±1` matrix `Q` with
`det Q = -1`) also qualify, and the engine handles both. Gaussian states
(here, `U|0^n⟩`) are fixed by their covariance matrix

    M_ab = -i ⟨γ_a γ_b⟩   (a ≠ b),   real antisymmetric, 2n x 2n,

with `⟨Z_k⟩ = M_{2k,2k+1}`. A gate acts as `M → Q M Qᵀ`. Higher moments follow
from Wick's theorem: `⟨γ_{a1} … γ_{a2m}⟩ = i^m Pf(M[a, a])`. So
`⟨Π_{k∈S} Z_k⟩ = Pf(M restricted to {2k, 2k+1 : k ∈ S})`.

**Matchgate condition.** A two-qubit gate `U` on modes `k, k+1` that are
adjacent in the order is Gaussian iff conjugation maps the four local
operators `c = (X⊗I, Y⊗I, Z⊗X, Z⊗Y)` into their span. The Jordan–Wigner
string to the left commutes with `U`. For a later mode the string contains
`Z_k Z_{k+1} = -c_0 c_1 c_2 c_3`, which `U` multiplies by `det Q`. So `Q` is
the identity outside the block's 4x4 part, except for the sign `det Q` on
every later Majorana operator. Equivalently, `U` is a matchgate: it acts on
the even (`|00⟩, |11⟩`) and odd (`|01⟩, |10⟩`) subspaces with blocks of equal
determinant. Parity-odd blocks such as `X ⊗ I` are also allowed. A one-qubit
gate is Gaussian iff it is diagonal (a phase `e^{iα n_k}`, a rotation of
`(γ_{2k}, γ_{2k+1})`) or anti-diagonal (`X`, `Y` up to phase: parity flips).
A diagonal two-qubit gate

    U = e^{iφ} e^{iα n_a} e^{iβ n_b} e^{i g n_a n_b}

is Gaussian iff the *interaction phase* `g ≡ 0 (mod 2π)`. Its one-site part
is Gaussian for any two wires, adjacent or not.

**SWAP vs fermionic SWAP.** A qubit `SWAP` is not Gaussian:
`SWAP = fSWAP · CZ`, and `CZ = exp(iπ n_a n_b)` is an interaction phase of
`π`. But a qubit `SWAP` is only a renaming of the wires. The detector applies
renamings to its wire labels instead of to the state, so `SWAP` gates and
whole SWAP networks cost nothing and introduce no signs: the Jordan–Wigner
order is chosen afterwards, on the renamed wires. The same reasoning
applies to any fused block of the form `SWAP · V` with `V` Gaussian or
diagonal. In particular `CZ = SWAP · fSWAP` is Gaussian up to a renaming.

**Number-conserving vs general.** Gates that conserve particle number
(hopping `XX + YY`, phases) have `Q` commuting with the complex structure
`J = ⊕ [[0, 1], [-1, 0]]`. The `n x n` complex correlation matrix
`⟨c†_i c_j⟩` then suffices. Pairing terms (`XX - YY`, `XY + YX`) and parity
flips need the full Majorana (Bogoliubov) form. The engine always uses the
Majorana form. Its cost is O(n) per block either way, only a constant
factor (about 4x in memory) larger than the correlation-matrix form. The
report states whether every Gaussian block is number-conserving
(`number_conserving`). This is a property of the blocks, not of the circuit:
an `X` that fusion leaves as a block of its own flips one occupation, so the
flag is false for the SU(2) circuits even though they conserve `N`. A separate correlation-matrix path is not
implemented.

## 2. What the detector checks

`gaussian::detect(circuit, &DetectOptions)` runs in O(gates · cut window²)
plus O(edges log edges) for the order.

1. **Relabel and fuse.** `SWAP` gates rename wires. Consecutive two-qubit
   gates on one pair fuse with the one-qubit gates between them into one
   4x4 block. One-qubit gates between blocks with different partners
   (*tails*) can belong to the previous block, the next one, or both: a
   basis change that closes one `XX` rotation can be followed by one that
   opens the next. For each block in time order the detector tries every
   cut point of its two closing tails (the first and last 8 for long tails)
   and keeps the best. The ranking is: Gaussian, then interaction-phase
   block, then the smallest residual, then the fewest gates taken. What is
   left opens the next block.
2. **Classify** each block: diagonal with `|g| ≤ tol` (one-site phases);
   matchgate; `SWAP ·` (phases or matchgate), turned into a renaming;
   diagonal with an interaction phase `g`; or non-Gaussian. The *residual*
   of a block is `max_i ||U† c_i U − Σ_j Q_ij c_j||_F / ||c_i||_F`, its
   distance from the Gaussian set. A three-qubit gate is an opaque
   non-Gaussian block with residual 1.
3. **Order.** Matchgates need adjacent modes. The detector keeps the qubit
   order if every matchgate pair is adjacent in it. Otherwise it orders the
   wires along the paths of the graph of matchgate pairs. Paths start at
   their smaller end and are sorted by first wire. If that graph has a
   vertex of degree > 2 or a cycle, it uses a greedy maximum-weight path
   cover instead (`Ordering::GreedyCover`). It keeps whichever order leaves
   more blocks adjacent. A matchgate whose wires end up non-adjacent counts
   as non-Gaussian with residual 1 (`nonadjacent`).
4. **Report** (`GaussianReport`): `gaussian_fraction` (Gaussian blocks / all
   blocks), `max_residual` (over non-interaction blocks), the interaction
   phases (`interactions`: block, wires, modes, `g`; `interaction_total =
   Σ|g|`, `interaction_max`), `nonadjacent`, `swaps_relabelled`, the order
   and its paths, `mode_of_qubit` (where each qubit's content ends up),
   `number_conserving`, `free` (Gaussian up to interaction phases) and
   `exact` (Gaussian, the engine applies). The tolerance is `tol = 1e-10`,
   so `exact` means "Gaussian to round-off".

`simulability::features` carries the summary (`gauss_fraction`,
`gauss_residual`, `gauss_interaction`, `gauss_exact`).

## 3. The engine and the planner

`GaussianState::from_circuit` compiles the blocks into O(2n) maps on 2 or 4
Majorana indices and evolves `M`, O(n) per block. The parity sign on all
later modes is kept lazily in a sign vector, so parity-odd blocks are O(n)
too. Read-outs, all in terms of the circuit's qubits at the end:

| read-out | method | cost |
|---|---|---|
| `⟨Z_i⟩` | `M_{2k,2k+1}` | O(1) |
| `⟨Z_i Z_j⟩`, any Z product | Wick: Pfaffian of the `2m x 2m` minor | O(m³) |
| `p(x)`, marginals | `Pf((M_S + B_x)/2)`, `B_x` the basis state's covariance | O(n³) |
| samples | mode-by-mode projection, rank-2 update of the rest | O(n³) per shot |
| amplitudes | not supported: `M` does not carry the global phase | — |

A phase-aware overlap (Pfaffian formulas with a tracked reference amplitude)
would give amplitudes. It is not implemented, so the planner never chooses
this engine for amplitude requests.

The planner (v2) runs the detector as tier "1g" when the circuit has no
three-qubit gate and the engine's lower bound beats the best prediction so
far by `voi` × the detector's cost. If the report is `exact`,
`Engine::Gaussian` joins the ranking with predicted time
`fixed + evolve · blocks · n` plus the read-out
(`pfaffian · (2m)³` for an m-fold Z product, `shot · (2n)³` per sample).
Unlike the other models, these constants are not fitted. They are set from
one timing on the development VPS (one core, the `opt-level = 1` test profile):
on the SU(2) circuits the detector takes about 1e-6 s per gate and the
covariance update about 3.6e-8 s per block per mode. The model uses 2e-8,
which assumes release builds are somewhat faster; that assumption has not
been measured. The constants are good to an order of magnitude. For the
free-fermion circuits it accepts, the engine is polynomial and every other
engine is exponential or bond-limited, so that precision is enough.
Detection plus evolution of one SU(2) circuit (120 qubits, ~30 000 gates,
3680 blocks) takes about 0.05 s in that profile.

## 4. Limits

* **Exactness.** The engine is exact only for `exact` circuits. Interaction
  phases can be dropped (`InteractionPolicy::Drop`), which gives the
  free-fermion part of the circuit. That is an approximation whose error
  grows with `Σ|g|`, and the caller must opt in.
* **Orders.** One Jordan–Wigner order per circuit, found from the block
  graph. Hopping on a ring (periodic boundary) or any graph that is not a
  union of paths, after renaming, is rejected. A ring term in JW form
  carries the parity operator. Supporting it needs parity-sector
  bookkeeping, or fermionic SWAPs that the detector would have to
  synthesise, not just recognise.
* **Fusion is greedy.** Tails are split between neighbouring blocks by a
  local search with a window of 8 gates at each end. A decomposition that
  needs a basis change to travel through a third block is not found. Gates
  in a cut-point window longer than 8 are tried only at the ends.
* **Non-gate operations** (measurement, reset, noise, conditionals): the
  detector ignores them and the engine refuses them (terminal measurements
  are stripped by the Python layer).
* **Read-outs.** Z-basis quantities only. X/Y Pauli terms are rotated to Z
  by Hadamard/S gates, which are not Gaussian on their own. Bilinears such
  as `⟨X_k X_{k+1}⟩` are Gaussian observables, but they are not exposed.
* **Not implemented.** Perturbative corrections in the interaction phases
  are not implemented. `GaussianState::evolve_with(program, max_bytes,
  on_interaction)` is the intended attachment point: it calls the hook with
  the state just before each interaction phase, in time order, then drops
  the phase.

## 5. Validation

`tests/engines/gaussian.rs`:

* random matchgate circuits, n ≤ 12: phases, `X`/`Y` flips, all four
  quadratic pair terms with CNOTs in both orientations, iSWAPs, `SWAP; CZ`
  fermionic swaps, and hopping. They are checked against the state-vector
  engine to 1e-10 on every `⟨Z_i⟩`, every `⟨Z_i Z_j⟩`, random Z products of
  up to 5 qubits, and basis-state probabilities. The same circuits are also
  run on a permuted qubit layout (found again as `Ordering::Paths`) and
  behind random SWAP networks. A sampling check uses 20 000 shots.
* rejection of a lone Hadamard, CNOT, `Rx`, a Toffoli, hopping on a ring
  and on a star, and a mid-circuit measurement. Interaction phases are
  reported, refused by default and dropped only on request.
* the planner choosing `Engine::Gaussian` at n = 48, for expectations and
  samples but not amplitudes, and its debug reference at n = 10.
* the SU(2) hadron-dynamics circuits (copied into `tests/data/su2-hadron`
  from the quantum advantage tracker at commit `1db844f`), with interaction
  phases dropped. The detector finds two 60-site chains (`Ordering::Paths`),
  1200 interaction phases with `|g| ≤ 0.01`, and `max_residual` below
  1e-12. It reproduces the free-fermion values of
  research/data/su2-hadron (`stag_SCV = -2.641078`,
  `n_f = stag_meson − stag_SCV = 0.118038`, `N = 60`) to 1e-6.
